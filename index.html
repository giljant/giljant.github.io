Process

From brief to launch

  1. Brief

    Talk with the client about the business, the audience and what the site has to achieve.

  2. Structure

    Turn their raw content into a sitemap, the sections of each page and the data model behind them.

  3. Design

    Design desktop and mobile in Figma, present it, and iterate through feedback rounds.

  4. Build

    Build the front end in React and Next.js, and the REST or GraphQL API and database behind it.

  5. Test & launch

    Test across devices and browsers, tune SEO and load speed, ship through Docker and CI/CD to the cloud, then maintain.

Web designs.

Two website designs I made for Croatian companies. Neither is the company’s live site. They show how I structure pages, plan for search and lay out mobile on its own terms.

01

Design proposal2026Construction

LTG a website proposal for a general contractor

LTG is a Croatian construction company with more than 30 years of work, from shell construction and facades to turnkey handover. I designed a proposal for their website: one site that shows the full range of what they do, proves they can be trusted with large buildings and turns visitors into quote requests. It is not their live site.

Role
Website design
Tool

Scope
Desktop + mobile
Shown
Homepage, Facade services

What I did

  1. Built the homepage around their promise of one contractor and one point of responsibility, with a dusk site photo and three proof points right in the hero.
  2. Designed the facade page as the template for every service page: a comparison of ETICS, ventilated and aluminium-glass systems, reasons to choose LTG, references, FAQ and a short quote form.
  3. Took the palette from the job site itself: charcoal, concrete grey and a rust orange used only for calls to action.

STEM-online a proposal for tutoring pages built for local search

STEM-online connects school and university students with tutors from Zagreb’s top technical faculties, in person or online. My proposal is a set of pages that answer local searches like “maths tutoring in Zagreb” and make booking a tutor take as few steps as possible. It is not their live site.

What I did

  1. Designed one landing page template that works for every subject and city combination, with breadcrumbs that mirror the URL path for search engines.
  2. Designed tutor cards with faculty and neighbourhood tags, so a student can judge a match at a glance.
  3. Built the Croatia overview page around a city picker and a four-step booking explainer for in-person and online lessons.
